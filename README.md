## How the Special Buy of the Day actually works

The deal of the day is a daily online sale with a hard reset time: **all offers reset at 3 AM ET** — which works out to 12 AM PST, 1 AM MST, and 2 AM CST. The countdown clock on the page runs on Eastern Standard Time, so if you're on the West Coast checking at 9 PM, the current deal still has five hours left.

A few ground rules from the official page matter more than they first appear:

- **Online only.** These prices won't match in-store, and you can't price-match them at a physical location.
- **While supplies last.** Popular tool and appliance deals can sell out before the timer hits zero. The official page says it outright: you have to visit frequently to catch the good ones.
- **Free delivery excludes major appliances.** If today's deal is a refrigerator or range, budget for delivery separately.
- **Pro Special Buy of the Week** runs on a separate track — a weekly bundle of savings aimed at contractors, refreshed once a week rather than daily.

If you don't want to check the page every morning, Home Depot runs a daily sale mailing list that sends the day's deals to your inbox. That's the official suggestion for catching deals "first," which matters when stock is the limiting factor.

## The fine print that can void your cash back

Cash back on Home Depot purchases comes with conditions, and several of them are the kind you only find by reading the terms. These are worth knowing before checkout, not after:

1. **Start with an empty shopping cart.** This is an explicit requirement. If items are already sitting in your cart when you click through from TopCashback, tracking can fail.
2. **Cash back is calculated on item price only.** Taxes, shipping and other fees don't earn anything — on a big appliance order, that's a meaningful chunk of the total excluded.
3. **Resale and bulk buying is ineligible.** Buying multiples with the intention of reselling can get the cash back declined outright. This matters for Pro shoppers stocking up on daily deals.
4. **Skip outside coupon codes.** A code you found on a third-party site can break the tracking link, especially if it bounces you off the retailer's page or opens a new tab. Deals found directly on Home Depot's own site are the safer bet.
5. **Don't use Safari.** TopCashback's own guidance notes that Safari can't be guaranteed to track properly. Chrome or another browser is the reliable route.
6. **File claims within 100 days.** If your cash back doesn't track automatically, you have 100 days from the order to submit a Missing Cash Back Claim.

None of these are dealbreakers. They're just the difference between cash back that posts and cash back that silently never shows up.
